Front-end design schemas · profiles · element standards

Three visual directions for the same spine, a profile model that resists vanity, and the standardized element list any of them must implement. Companion to the prototype, the sell sheet, and the builder's playbook.

01What the front end must say before it says anything else

Every screen answers one question first: is this thing alive, and how alive? Life state outranks author, content, and chrome. The three schemas below disagree about mood and audience. They agree about that hierarchy, because the moment a card reads as "a post" before it reads as "a dying ember," the interface has left the product.

The interface rule of the wall: life state is always visible, always legible without color alone, and always honest. No screen hides a lifeline, softens a death, or dresses a countdown as decoration.

02Three design schemas

Each schema states its claim, what it gives up, its tokens and type, a signature element, and two live samples: a post card (healthy and critical) and the profile treatment. Pick one as the identity. The other two remain honest alternatives, and Tinder & Ash doubles as the print/marketing skin whichever way you go.

Hearthlight

the warm dark room · default recommendation

Claim: the app is a room lit by the things burning in it. Warm dark surfaces, light that comes from posts rather than chrome, glow proportional to life. It gives up: daylight neutrality. This skin commits to atmosphere, and spreadsheet-minded users will read it as a place rather than a tool.

Tinder & Ash

paper light · the color budget schema

Claim: spend the entire color budget on mortality. Chrome, text, and layout stay achromatic paper and char; the only saturated color anywhere on screen is life state. A glance at any distance shows you what is alive. It gives up: atmosphere. It reads as a quiet reading tool, which suits long-form communities and doubles as the print identity.

Signalfire

the night watch · instrument-forward

Claim: the feed is a watchtower. Cold night surfaces, one signal color, and data surfaced as first-class UI: decay rates, distinct-keeper counts, regime readouts. Built for the community that will eventually want the open zone. It gives up: softness. Casual users can find it clinical, so it fits best as the opt-in "open zone" skin layered over Hearthlight rather than the default.

03The profile model all three schemas share

Whatever the skin, a profile is a hearth someone keeps, not a trophy wall. The information order is fixed; only the dress changes.

1 · Identity and standing

  • Handle, avatar, standing (grade + party-lift), epoch mark. Founder marks render first and cannot be purchased or transferred.
  • Standing is the only rank shown. No follower count anywhere on the primary surface.

2 · Titles that work

  • Earned titles render as chips with their mechanical effect one tap away. A title with no effect does not ship.
  • Order: founder marks, then ascension titles, then milestone titles.

3 · Burning now

  • The person's living posts with live lifelines. This is the profile's centerpiece: what they have at stake right now.
  • Empty state invites action: "Nothing burning. Light something."

4 · Tending record, then ash

  • Contribution stats lead: saves given, branches grown, fires kept alive past their maker's last visit. Authorship stats follow.
  • The ash record closes the page. Deaths are stated plainly with duration, never hidden. Mortality is history, not failure.

04Standardized element list

The canonical UI vocabulary. Every schema implements these under its own dress. Tier matches the playbook: cut a SPINE element and the interface no longer tells the truth about the model.

SPINE must ship, phase 1  ·  CORE ships with its mechanic, phase 2  ·  OPT optional layer
ElementWhat it communicates & rules of useDesign guidance
LifelineSPINE The post's remaining life. Always visible on every card, every context, no exceptions. Never color-only: pair with percent or time so color-blind users read the same truth. One glyph per schema (wick, ring, rail). Position fixed across the whole app so the eye learns one place to look.
Time-to-deathSPINE Absolute remaining time. Switches to minutes under one hour, adds the critical state under 15%. Rounds honestly, never dramatizes. Utility mono, adjacent to the lifeline. Critical state may pulse once per breath; respect reduced-motion by swapping pulse for a static badge.
Save actionSPINE The core verb. Shows its ember cost before tap and confirms the life added after. Largest interactive target on the card. Label stays "Save" through the whole flow. The only button rendered in the life color at full saturation. Everything else yields.
Clutch flareSPINE Marks the clutch window (life under 15%) and celebrates a clutch save. The skill moment of the whole app; the celebration credits the saver by handle. The one place per schema where motion spends its budget. Cool spark tone (blue-white) so it reads as rescue, never as alarm.
Keeper countSPINE Distinct people sustaining the post. Shown as people, never as raw repost volume, because distinct care is the number the model actually runs on. "14 keepers" reads better than "14 reposts." Tap opens the roll of who showed up, newest first.
Decay pillSPINE The post's effective decay multiplier (×0.96, ×1.31). Folds every modifier into one honest number. Warm color above 1, cool below, neutral at 1. Tap explains the ingredients in plain words: "Storm +0.8 · deepburn −0.2." Transparency is the feature.
Canonical pointerSPINE On any reshare card: routes to the one true original. A reshare never renders as a standalone copy. "↳ to @riverkeeper's post" as a persistent header line on every derived card.
Ash cardSPINE A dead post's marker: duration lived, keepers at death, epoch. Content is gone; the record remains. Death renders as history, not error. Desaturated to the schema's ash tone. No red. A death is an ending, not a failure state.
Ember meterCORE The player's regenerating action budget. Persistent but quiet; every action shows its cost at the point of action, not in a manual. Thin bar in the app chrome, never inside cards. When empty, actions gray out with a regen countdown instead of an error.
Grade badgeCORE The post's grade (Spark→Inferno) and therefore its life ceiling. Grade colors stay within the life ramp so the ladder reads as heat. Word plus glyph, not glyph alone. Ascension moment gets one celebratory beat, then returns to quiet.
Epoch chipCORE The regime the post was born in, and its buffering. Founder epochs render distinct (the one violet exception to every palette) because they cannot recur. ⟡ prefix reserved for epochs alone. Tap explains the buffer in one sentence.
Trait & title chipsCORE Earned mechanics that do work (deepburn, anchor, Clutch Hand). Every chip's effect is one tap away. A chip that does nothing does not ship. Small, mono, bordered. Cap visible chips at three plus overflow, so cards never turn into medal racks.
Branch nodeCORE A contribution with its own life, channeling upward. Renders nested under the canonical original with its own small lifeline. Indent one level only, then "view tree." The tree view is where branch depth lives; the feed stays shallow.
Standing displayCORE A person's grade plus party-lift ("Blaze +0.8"). The lift is visible and bounded so leadership reads as carried-up, never self-made. Handle-adjacent everywhere the handle appears. Tap shows who lifted them: the roster is the point.
Hearth readoutCORE Community-scoped health: epoch, density, current regime. Scoped to a room, never global, per the playbook's dashboard rule. Lives in the community header. A cooling room is a rally cue, so pair it with the room's most critical posts.
Reorder pillCORE Feed changed while reading. The feed never reorders under a reading user; new order arrives on tap. Floating pill, top center: "feed shifted · 4 new." Reading position is sacred.
Clash frameOPT Wraps a post that entered the open zone: opponent, stakes, weight class. Renders only inside the open zone. The safe hearth never shows clash chrome. Signalfire dress regardless of base schema, so crossing into the open zone reads as entering a different room.
Sprout markerOPT An orphaned branch rallying to take root: countdown plus fresh keepers needed. High-drama, short-lived. Borrow the clutch flare's spark tone. The "took root" moment credits every rallier.

05Global standards, whichever schema wins

Color speaks life, and only life

gold · full
ember · fading
red · critical
ash · dead
  • The life ramp is reserved. No decorative use of gold, ember, red, or ash anywhere else in the interface.
  • Two exceptions, both earned: spark blue for clutch rescue, epoch violet for founder marks.
  • State never rides on color alone. Percent, time, or a word always rides along.

Motion has one budget

  • Spend it on the clutch flare and ascension. Decay itself never animates in alarm; a dying post fades and slows, it does not flash.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion by swapping every animation for a static state change, and design that static state first.
  • No infinite ambient animation in the feed. Calm is a retention feature for an app about loss.

Numbers must do work

  • Every number on screen changes a decision: life, time, cost, keepers, decay. Ship no number that exists to be admired.
  • Contribution counts before authorship counts, everywhere, in every schema.
  • Follower counts do not exist on primary surfaces. Standing is the rank, and standing is earned through others.

Type roles stay fixed

  • Display serif (Fraunces) for identity moments: wordmark, grades, big durations. Used with restraint.
  • Grotesk for content and controls. Mono for every number the model produces, so "model truth" has a recognizable voice.
  • Post body may take the serif only in Tinder & Ash, where reading is the point.

Honesty rules

  • Deaths are never hidden, softened into "archived," or animated as punishment. A death is stated, dated, and kept.
  • Every mechanical state is inspectable in one tap, in plain words. If a tooltip needs jargon, rename the mechanic.
  • The interface never manufactures urgency the model did not produce. The countdown is the truth; it needs no help.

Accessibility floor

  • 4.5:1 contrast for text in every schema, including on the warm darks. Life-state pairs checked for the eight common CVD types.
  • Full keyboard path: save, add, clutch. Focus rings use the schema's spark or signal tone, never the life ramp.
  • Lifelines expose their value to screen readers as "life 68 percent, 2 hours 41 minutes remaining."

06Recommendation

Ship Hearthlight as the identity. It is the only schema that makes the density regime felt (a dim room is a starving room) and its warmth counterweights the app's central fact, which is loss. Hold Signalfire as the open-zone dress so entering the adversarial layer reads as walking out of the warm room into the night watch, and keep Tinder & Ash as the print and marketing skin, where its color-budget rule turns every screenshot into an argument for the product. One spine, three rooms, no dilution.
